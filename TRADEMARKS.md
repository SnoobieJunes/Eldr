# Trademarks and naming

> **Not legal advice.** This is the project's stated policy, written by the
> maintainers. If you are relying on it commercially, have your own counsel
> read it.

## The short version

**The code is open. The name is not.**

You may fork this project, modify it, and distribute it — that is what the
[AGPL-3.0](LICENSE) and [Apache-2.0](LICENSING.md) grants are for. You may not
distribute your fork **under our name**.

The AGPL-3.0 permits this reservation under section 7(e).

## What you may do

- **Use the software** for anything, commercially or not, per its license.
- **Say what your thing is compatible with.** Nominative, factual, honest
  reference is fine and always will be: "implements the PQRC protocol",
  "compatible with Eldr", "a fork of EldrChat". Do not imply endorsement,
  affiliation, or that we published your build.
- **Implement the protocol under your own name.** The specification, the NIP,
  the extension profiles, and the test vectors are **public domain (CC0-1.0)** —
  deliberately. Build a competing client. We would like you to.

## What you may not do

- Ship a fork to the App Store, Play Store, a package registry, or any other
  distribution channel **under the name Eldr, EldrChat, or Huginn**, or under a
  name or icon likely to be confused with them.
- Use the marks or logos in a way that suggests we endorse, maintain, sponsor,
  or vouch for your build.
- Register the marks, or confusingly similar ones, as a trademark, domain,
  company name, or app listing.

**Fork it, rename it, ship it.** That is the whole ask. Pick your own name and
icon and you are in the clear.

## Why this exists

The AGPL is often mistaken for a ban on redistribution. It is not — it permits
anyone to fork and ship these apps commercially, provided they publish complete
corresponding source under the AGPL. What actually prevents a rebadged clone of
*this app, with this name,* appearing next to the original in a store is
trademark, not copyright. This document is that boundary, stated plainly so
nobody has to guess.

It is deliberately narrow. It restricts **naming**, never **use**, **study**,
**modification**, or **redistribution** — so it takes nothing away from the
open-source grants and does not affect this project's OSI-compliant status.

Questions, or want permission for something this document does not cover? Open
an issue.
